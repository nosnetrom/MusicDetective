About {{ title() }}

{{ title() }} helps you dig into the people behind the music: who an artist is, what they've released, and who played what on each track. You can also click on any of those artists names to start a new search on them, and keep going down the rabbit hole. 🐰

Where the information comes from

Everything you see is pulled live from MusicBrainz (opens in a new tab), an open, community-maintained music encyclopedia run by the non-profit MetaBrainz Foundation. {{ title() }} doesn't keep its own copy of the data. Each search asks MusicBrainz directly at that moment, so you always get its latest information, including corrections other contributors made minutes ago.

How a search works

  1. Find the artist. Your search is sent to MusicBrainz, and {{ title() }} shows the best-matching artist or group, along with their active years where known.
  2. Gather their releases. Next, it requests up to 100 of that artist's releases. Only official releases issued in the United States or the United Kingdom are kept, listed from newest to oldest and then by title.
  3. Open a release for the credits. Choosing a release fetches its full track list, along with the musicians credited on each track and the instruments or vocal parts they performed. Where a release credits a specific instrument, such as a particular guitar, that detail appears in parentheses.

Why things sometimes take a moment

MusicBrainz is a free service, and to keep it running smoothly for everyone it allows each application about one request per second. {{ title() }} respects that limit by lining up its requests and sending them roughly a second apart. That's why the release list may appear a beat after the artist, and track details can take a moment when you open a release right after a search.

If MusicBrainz is especially busy, it may briefly turn a request away. When that happens, you'll see a message with a Try again button. Waiting a few seconds and trying again usually works.

A note on accuracy

MusicBrainz is built by volunteers, so the level of detail varies. Well-known albums often have complete track-by-track credits, while others may list only the tracks, or no musicians at all. If something is missing or wrong, it's missing or wrong at the source, and anyone can contribute corrections to MusicBrainz (opens in a new tab).
